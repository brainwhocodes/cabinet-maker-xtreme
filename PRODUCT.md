# Product

## Register

product

## Users

DIY homeowners planning a kitchen, laundry room, mudroom, or utility-room cabinet layout. They may have little cabinetry experience and need help turning room dimensions into a layout they can understand, purchase, assemble, and install. They often work on a laptop while planning and on a phone or tablet while assembling.

## Product Purpose

Create a browser-based cabinet planning workspace that converts measured walls and openings into a validated 3D layout built from a versioned catalog of stock or ready-to-assemble cabinet sizes. The product produces a retailer-ready bill of materials, installation supplies list, and a cabinet-specific step-by-step HTML assembly and installation guide. WebMCP lets a person direct an agent to inspect constraints, propose layouts, change the scene, explain warnings, produce the BOM, and guide assembly without forcing the agent to infer controls from pixels.

Success means a first-time DIY user can model one room, place or auto-fit stock cabinets, understand every warning, export a BOM with honest retailer links, and complete the guided assembly flow without needing to interpret the 3D canvas alone.

## Brand Personality

Precise, patient, practical. The product should feel like a well-made measuring tool paired with a calm shop teacher: technically credible, physically grounded, and friendly without becoming childish.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI SaaS dashboards with purple gradients, glass cards, glowing borders, decorative pills, and fake system jargon.
- Dense professional CAD interfaces that hide primary actions behind unlabeled icons or require prior drafting knowledge.
- Retailer-branded clones, including copied Home Depot or IKEA colors, logos, trade dress, characters, or instruction-sheet layouts.
- Toy-like 3D room planners where appearance is prioritized over dimensions, clearances, error recovery, or accessible equivalents.
- Placeholder illustrations, invented SVG icons, rasterized UI text, or generated imagery used where semantic HTML should carry the information.

## Design Principles

1. Dimensions before decoration. Every visual object maps to explicit catalog and geometry data.
2. Propose, preview, apply. Agent-generated layout changes remain inspectable and reversible before commitment.
3. Explain the next physical action. Warnings and assembly steps state what to do, what is needed, and why it matters.
4. Canvas plus equivalent structure. Every essential 3D action and fact also exists in keyboard-accessible semantic UI.
5. Honest commerce. Retailer results distinguish verified product data, search links, estimates, timestamps, and unavailable prices.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support complete keyboard operation, visible focus, 44px touch targets, text zoom, high contrast, reduced motion, and color-independent status. The Three.js canvas is an enhancement, never the only way to inspect or edit a design; maintain a synchronized semantic cabinet list, numeric position controls, and readable validation summary. Assembly instructions use headings, ordered steps, real lists, descriptive figure alternatives, print styles, and persistent progress. WebMCP complements rather than replaces semantic HTML and assistive-technology support.
